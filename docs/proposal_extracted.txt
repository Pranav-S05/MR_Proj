   Calibrated Confidence for Kilometre-Scale Feed-Forward SLAM
                               Project Timeline Document, Mobile Robotics
                                Kavish Vora, Pranav Srivastava, Mehrish Khan
                       International Institute of Information Technology, Hyderabad

Abstract

Feed-forward 3D models such as VGGT process multiple images jointly and return camera poses,
depth estimates, and per-pixel confidence in a single forward pass. Processing thousands of frames
jointly is impractical at the memory budgets available to us. VGGT-Long therefore processes the
sequence in overlapping windows and connects neighbouring windows usingSim(3)alignments. This
allows the local reconstructions to be chained into trajectories spanning several kilometres, despite the
model operating on relatively short windows. In the pose graph, however, every connection between
windows is trusted equally, even though some are much worse than others and the network reports
a confidence for every pixel. Our project does three things. First, we measure VGGT’s confidence
against real depth and pose error on KITTI, using LiDAR and ground-truth poses. Second, if the
confidence is off, we fit a simple calibration (isotonic regression, no training) that converts it into an
uncertainty for every edge of the pose graph. Third, we feed these uncertainties to the VGGT-Long
back-end and compare ATE (Sim(3)-aligned RMSE) on KITTI, Waymo and TUM RGB-D against
uniform weights and VGGT-Long’s own weighting, at the same window size. We also test loop-closure
edges and deliberately injected false loops.

1  Motivation

Background.VGGT [1] attends over all frames at once, so its memory grows quickly with the
number of frames, and on our GPUs it handles only tens of frames. VGGT-Long [2] gets around this
by running the frozen model on overlapping windows. It aligns each window to the next using the
frames they share. Each window has its own unknown scale (a single camera cannot measure metres),
so the alignment is a similarity transform,Sim(3), not a rigid one. Loops found by place recognition
are added, and a pose graph is solved. Each window on its own is accurate. The drift comes from
theseamsbetween windows. Every seam adds a small rotation, translation and scale error, and a
few hundred seams add up to tens of metres. The pose graph is where this error gets spread out and
corrected.
We see three shortcomings in the current approach:

S1.All edges are weighted the same.VGGT-Long uses confidence to weight pointsinsideeach
     alignment. Its global objective, however, is a plain sum of squared residuals, so every seam and
     every loop edge gets an identity information matrix, to our reading of [2]. Real seams vary in
     quality: they get worse with low texture, sharp turns, moving cars and small overlap. The 7
     degrees of freedom also differ in reliability, and scale drift is the classic failure of monocular
     SLAM [3]. Equal weighting gives unreliable edges the same influence on the global solution as
     well-constrained edges, potentially causing the optimizer to distribute corrections inappropriately.
S2.Confidence has not been checked at the level a pose graph needs.DUSt3R, MASt3R
     and VGGT [1,4,5] learn confidenceCwith a loss of the formCe−αlogC[6], whereeis the
     prediction error. This loss is minimised atC=α/E[e], so a perfectly trained model would satisfy
     E[e|C] =α/C. Existing audits work per pixel: Hillemann et al. [7] on DTU, and the calibration
     audit of N. Deng et al. [8] on seven backbones and 13 datasets including KITTI, who find that
     confidence ranks error well but understates its magnitude and propose a two-constant power-law
     correction.  To our knowledge, no prior work has tested for the quantities a SLAM back-end
     consumes, namely window-to-windowSim(3)seams and loop closures, or whether correcting it
     changes the trajectory.

S3.Pixel confidence does not directly give an edge uncertainty.A seam is estimated from
     about10       4 pixels. If those pixels are treated as independent and their information is added up,
     the seam looks far more certain than it is. Their errors are strongly correlated because they come
     from one forward pass with one shared scale. There is currently no tested way to turn per-pixel
     confidence into the7×7uncertainty a pose graph edge needs.

2  Related Work

Feed-forward geometry.DUSt3R[4]andMASt3R[5]predict3Dpointmapswithconfidenceforim-
agepairs. VGGT[1]handlesmanyviewsandalsopredictscameras. CUT3R[9]andStreamVGGT[10]
process video as a stream with a persistent memory. VGGT-Ω[11] adds scene-summarising register
tokens and trains with less memory, but to our knowledge is not evaluated on long driving sequences.
Long sequences.VGGT-Long [2], described above, is our baseline. VGGT-SLAM [12] argues that
Sim(3)alignment is not enough for uncalibrated cameras and aligns submaps with 15-DOF SL(4)
transforms instead. We keepSim(3)to match our baseline; our weighting method would carry over
to SL(4) unchanged. MASt3R-SLAM [13] uses MASt3R confidences as weights in its back-end, but
does not check whether they are calibrated. DROID-SLAM [14] learns its confidence weights end to
end, which needs training.Sim(3)pose graphs for monocular scale drift go back to Strasdat et al. [3].
Uncertainty and robust back-ends.Kuleshov et al. [15] measure calibration of regression models
with reliability diagrams and fix it with isotonic regression; we follow their method. The calibration
auditofN.Dengetal.[8](nottheVGGT-Longauthors)checksnativeconfidenceonsevenfeed-forward
backbones and 13 datasets, finding good error ranking but miscalibrated uncertainty magnitude and
proposing apost-hoc power-law correction. DCS [16]protects pose graphs againstwrong loop closures,
and we reuse its false-loop testing protocol.

3  Contributions

Each contribution addresses one shortcoming from Section 1:
C1.Confidence audit on outdoor driving data (addresses S2).We will extend pixel-level
      audits to window-to-window seams and loop closures, while retaining the per-pixel LiDAR check
      as a replication. We will compare VGGT’s confidence with real error at both levels, using ground-
      truth relative poses for edges. We will publish reliability diagrams and test theE[e|C]∝1/C
      relation directly.
C2.Calibrated edge uncertainties without training (addresses S1 and S3).We will learn a
      simple monotone map from seam-level confidence statistics to a standard deviation for rotation,
      translation and scale. Together these give a7×7information matrix for every seam and loop
      edge. The map is fitted in seconds on a CPU and plugs into the VGGT-Long back-end without
      other changes.
C3.Systematic evaluation across domains and failure modes.We will compare seven weight-
      ing schemes, three robust kernels, and loops on and off, at equal window size on held-out KITTI
      sequences,WaymoandTUMRGB-D.ThecomparisonalsoincludesATEagainstsequencelength,
      a false-loop stress test, and a memory and speed study on 8–11GB GPUs. Since the method
      only reads cached model outputs, other engines can be dropped in: as bonus experiments we test
      streaming models (CUT3R, StreamVGGT) and VGGT-Ω(Step 7).

Step 6: False-loop stress test.Following [16], we addk∈{0,1,2,4,8,16}false loops between
distant windows, with 20 random seeds. The test has two versions. In theadversarialversion, every
fake loop gets the same high weight, which tests only the robust kernels. In therealisticversion, we
lower the place-recognition threshold until real wrong matches appear, and each scheme weights them
by its own rule; this version shows whether calibration helps. A run survives if its trajectory error
stays within 1.25×the error with no false loops.
Step 7 (bonus): Streaming models and VGGT-Ω.If time allows: (i) we run CUT3R [9] (fixed-
size state) and StreamVGGT [10] (cached keys and values) on the whole sequence in one stream and
in windows with our back-end, and compare ATE against sequence length, revisit error and peak
GPU memory, to see whether a persistent state removes the need for chunking or only hides the drift.
(ii) VGGT-Ω[11] has released checkpoints; if they run on our GPUs, we use the 1B reproduction
checkpoint, which its authors retrained for benchmarking. It returns depth, depth confidence, camera
poses and per-frame register tokens, so it drops into our pipeline unchanged. It still cannot take a full
KITTI sequence in one pass (its authors report 43GB for 500 frames), to our knowledge is not tested
on long driving data, and its confidence has, to our knowledge, not been audited. We (a) run the
same confidence audit and check whether the VGGT calibration transfers or must be refit, (b) test its
register tokens, which summarise the scene, as the loop-detection feature against the DINOv2-based
model, and (c) run the full pipeline with it as the engine at the same memory budget.

5  Datasets

KITTI odometry[19] is our main dataset. Sequences 00–10 have ground-truth poses (271 to 4,661
frames, up to about 5km), colour images, Velodyne LiDAR and calibration, and VGGT-Long reports
results on them. We fix the split before any experiment. The calibration is fitted on05 and 07,
which have loops. Results are reported on the held-out00 and 02(main, long and with loops) and
on the other sequences. Leave-one-sequence-out runs check that the result does not depend on the
split. The 23.2k frames take one inference pass on the lab 2080Ti and Kaggle T4 GPUs, after which
all experiments run on the CPU.
Waymo Open[20] is our second outdoor dataset. Its segments are about 20s long (about 200 frames
at 10Hz), with LiDAR and accurate poses. Because the full dataset is very large, we download only
the front-camera images, poses and LiDAR for a fixed subset of validation segments, chosen before any
experiment (VGGT-Long’s segments where they are listed). Waymo adds a second driving domain to
the depth audit and tests whether the KITTI calibration transfers.
TUM RGB-D[21] is our indoor dataset: handheld, with motion-capture ground truth (fr1/desk,
fr1/room, fr2/xyz, fr3/long_office_household).  We use it to test whether the KITTI calibration
transfers to a very different setting without refitting.

Fairness rules.All schemes use the same cached outputs, seams, loop candidates, window size and
solver settings. The identity-weight solver must reproduce VGGT-Long’s own back-end result. Only
the weights (and, separately, the robust kernel) change. No ground truth from the test sequences is
used in any fitting, except by the oracle (f), an upper bound, not a method. Main configurations are
repeated under 3–5 window offsets and reported as mean±std.
Possible outcomes.(1) Calibrated weights improve ATE or robustness relative to uniform weights
and VGGT-Long’s weighting. (2) No ATE gain, but the oracle shows how much room weighting has,
and the robustness or residual-based results still stand. (3) Raw confidence already ranks seam error
well and only its level is off, which the back-end ignores; we then report where calibration still matters,
including robust-kernel thresholds, loops versus seams, and DoF balance.
